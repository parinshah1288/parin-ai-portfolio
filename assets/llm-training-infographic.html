Training Large Language Models

From collecting the first dataset to serving a model, with a look at the resources behind each step.

How a model gets trained and deployed

  1. 1Collect data

    Select and document sources such as web text, code and licensed material. Check usage rights and coverage.

  2. 2Prepare the dataset

    Remove duplicates, filter low-quality and sensitive content, tokenize the text and hold out evaluation data.

  3. 3Pretrain the model

    Predict the next token, measure the error and update model weights through backpropagation across accelerators.

  4. 4Post-train the model

    Tune on instruction examples and use preference feedback to improve how the model follows requests.

  5. 5Evaluate and red-team

    Test on held-out tasks and probe for harmful outputs, bias and privacy leakage. Revisit earlier stages if needed.

  6. 6Deploy and monitor

    Serve a versioned model, track latency and output quality, and use incidents and feedback to inform updates.

Evaluation can send a team back to data preparation or post-training before release.

What drives resource use and cost

Data

Licensing, storage, filtering and annotation cost money. Coverage and quality shape what the model learns and where it struggles.

Compute

Accelerators, networking and storage support parallel training. More work or lower utilization raises accelerator-hours and can increase cost.

Energy

Accelerators, servers and cooling draw electricity. Energy cost depends on measured kWh and the local rate. Facility overhead adds to device use.

Time

Data preparation, training, evaluation and recovery all affect the delivery schedule. Checkpointing limits repeated work after failures.

Where resources meet the workflow · Data quality affects pretraining and evaluation. Compute use and power draw drive energy use. Hardware throughput and restarts affect elapsed time.
