LLM Training Infographic Commentary

Parin Shah · AIML-501 Model Development

What the Graphic Shows

The workflow follows a model from data collection to deployment. Pretraining builds general language capabilities by learning to predict the next token. Post-training improves instruction following. Evaluation checks whether those improvements hold up on unseen examples and under adverse conditions. Monitoring continues after release.

How to Read the Numbers

Meta reports 15.6 trillion pretraining tokens and 30.84 million H100 GPU-hours for Llama 3.1 405B. GPU-hours add up time across devices, so Meta’s documented 54-day observation window is a different measure. Multiplying GPU-hours by an assumed constant 0.7 kW gives about 21.6 GWh for the GPUs in that scenario. The calculation excludes servers and cooling and uses the rated power as an assumption. Stanford’s GPT-4 and Gemini Ultra figures estimate compute spending. Data rights, staff, experiments and serving can add separate costs.

How I Classified the Information

I grouped actions by what they produce. Data preparation produces a usable dataset, pretraining produces base weights, and evaluation provides evidence for a release decision. I grouped resources by what teams have to budget. For example, labeling belongs under data even though it takes staff time, and GPU-hours measure compute even though the unit contains hours. I kept elapsed time separate because adding GPUs can change the schedule without reducing total compute.

Reflection on Learning

The hardest part was comparing figures that look similar at first glance. I initially read GPU-hours as a duration. Checking Meta’s cluster size and the scope of its 54-day observation helped me understand why those numbers answer different questions. This also connected the course’s discussion of optimization with my experience in distributed systems. I would now ask for accelerator-hours, elapsed time and metered electricity separately when assessing a training plan.

Design Rationale

I used a numbered flow to make the sequence easy to follow and a separate panel for the four resources so readers can see which costs recur across stages. The horizontal bars compare two estimates from the same report on a shared dollar scale. Llama’s reported activity and the illustrative energy calculation sit in their own panel because they use different units. Direct labels and high-contrast text make the graphic readable without relying on color.
