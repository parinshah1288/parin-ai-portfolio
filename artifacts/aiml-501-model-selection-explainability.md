<section class="hero"><p class="label">AIML-501 / Model Development / Portfolio Artifact 5</p><h2>Which pre-trained model should we deploy?</h2><p>An evidence-based selection matrix for language, computer vision, and tabular AI. Compare quality against the constraints that matter in a real system: inference efficiency, footprint, interpretability, and operational risk.</p><div class="chips"><span>5 pre-trained models</span><span>3 application domains</span><span>Benchmarks cited</span><span>Explainability-aware</span></div></section>
<section aria-labelledby="introduction"><p class="label">01 / Introduction</p><h2 id="introduction">Why explainability belongs in model selection</h2><p>A leaderboard winner may still be unsuitable for a time-sensitive application or one where users must understand a consequential prediction. High accuracy can come with increased compute, memory, and inference time; smaller models often make sense when service-level objectives and cost matter. Explainability helps teams inspect how predictions respond to input features, but post-hoc explanations must be tested for stability and fidelity rather than assumed to be causal accounts.</p><p>I selected five identifiable pre-trained checkpoints: <strong>all-MiniLM-L6-v2</strong> and <strong>all-mpnet-base-v2</strong> for NLP embeddings, <strong>MobileNetV3 Large</strong> and <strong>EfficientNet-B0</strong> for image classification, and <strong>TabPFN-v2</strong> for tabular classification. XGBoost, LightGBM, and Random Forest remain valuable tabular baselines, but they are ordinarily trained on the target dataset and are not treated here as pre-trained foundation models.</p></section>

<section aria-labelledby="method"><p class="label">02 / Methodology</p><h2 id="method">Selection and measurement rules</h2><div class="grid"><div class="panel nlp"><h3>Model size</h3><p>Parameter count and stored weight footprint. Peak memory additionally includes activations, caches, input batches, and runtime overhead.</p></div><div class="panel vision"><h3>Accuracy and quality</h3><p>Compare models on the <strong>same task and benchmark</strong>: semantic-search aggregate score for NLP; ImageNet-1K top-1 for vision; multi-dataset classification evidence for tabular data.</p></div><div class="panel nlp"><h3>Speed and compute</h3><p>NLP uses catalog-reported sentences per second; vision uses GFLOPs per image, a <strong>compute proxy and not latency</strong>; tabular uses a published example of wall-clock inference time.</p></div><div class="panel tabular"><h3>Explainability and integration</h3><p>Assess available attributions, evidence traceability, input requirements, licensing, the deployment environment, monitoring, and human-review requirements.</p></div></div><p class="small">This is a source-based comparative analysis, not a new laboratory benchmark or a claim that I measured these models. The quality, throughput, and compute metrics use different scales across domains, so a combined numerical ranking would be misleading.</p></section>

<section aria-labelledby="analysis"><p class="label">04 / Recommendations</p><h2 id="analysis">Choosing by application scenario</h2><div class="grid3"><div class="panel nlp"><p class="tag">NLP / high-volume semantic retrieval</p><h3>Choose MiniLM</h3><p>For fast document matching, semantic search, and embedding pipelines with tight budgets. Confirm recall@k and relevance on your corpus.</p></div><div class="panel nlp"><p class="tag">NLP / search relevance</p><h3>Choose MPNet</h3><p>When the reported 7.48-point semantic-search advantage is worth larger weights and lower throughput. Validate downstream business relevance.</p></div><div class="panel vision"><p class="tag">Vision / edge devices</p><h3>Choose MobileNetV3</h3><p>When per-image compute and device constraints dominate. Measure batch-1 p95 latency on the actual accelerator.</p></div><div class="panel vision"><p class="tag">Vision / image quality</p><h3>Choose EfficientNet-B0</h3><p>For a higher reference top-1 score when more compute is acceptable. Fewer parameters do not necessarily mean faster inference.</p></div><div class="panel tabular"><p class="tag">Tabular / limited labels</p><h3>Consider TabPFN-v2</h3><p>For small labeled classification data and rapid baseline development; compare to CatBoost and XGBoost and check memory, latency, and calibration.</p></div><div class="panel tabular"><p class="tag">Risk-sensitive decisions</p><h3>Require evidence</h3><p>A model must pass explainability, error-cost, subgroup fairness, and auditability checks—not merely have the largest benchmark score.</p></div></div></section>
<section aria-labelledby="explain"><p class="label">05 / Explainability</p><h2 id="explain">Explanations must be tested, not trusted blindly</h2><div class="grid3"><div class="panel nlp"><h3>Text embeddings</h3><p>Inspect retrieved neighbors, counterexamples, token perturbations, and performance on specialized or multilingual language. Similarity is not a causal rationale.</p></div><div class="panel vision"><h3>Computer vision</h3><p>Inspect Grad-CAM and saliency overlays and test whether they remain stable under small nonsemantic changes. Heatmaps expose associations, not causal certainty.</p></div><div class="panel tabular"><h3>Tabular prediction</h3><p>Use permutation importance, SHAP-style explanations, partial-dependence plots, and counterfactual review. Correlated predictors may make attributions unstable.</p></div></div></section>
<section aria-labelledby="workflow"><p class="label">06 / Engineering workflow</p><h2 id="workflow">From research to reliable deployment</h2><div class="steps"><div class="step"><strong>1 / Specify</strong><p>Set accuracy, p95 latency, memory, licensing, and error-cost requirements.</p></div><div class="step"><strong>2 / Benchmark</strong><p>Test candidates against representative held-out data using identical hardware and preprocessing.</p></div><div class="step"><strong>3 / Explain</strong><p>Audit hard cases, feature attributions, calibration, subgroup errors, and stability.</p></div><div class="step"><strong>4 / Monitor</strong><p>Track operational latency, cost, drift, and failures after deployment.</p></div></div></section>
<section aria-labelledby="conclusion"><p class="label">07 / Conclusion & design rationale</p><h2 id="conclusion">There is no universal best model</h2><p>The language example reveals a clear quality-versus-throughput trade-off; the vision example shows why parameter count alone cannot predict computational speed; and TabPFN illustrates how pre-training can accelerate small-data modeling but introduce inference costs. The best choice depends on application needs and the consequences of mistakes.</p><p>I used a consistent five-column visual matrix and color-coded its domains—teal for NLP, violet for vision, and orange for tabular data. The accompanying accessible table, explicit units, source notes, scenario recommendations, and validation workflow help readers interpret the graphic without inventing a cross-domain accuracy ranking. <a href="/parin-ai-portfolio/artifacts/aiml-501-model-selection-explanatory-note">Read the companion explanatory note.</a></p></section>
