<section class="xai-hero" aria-labelledby="xai-title"><div class="xai-kicker">AIML-501 / Model Development / Portfolio Artifact 4</div><h2 id="xai-title">Explainable AI:<br>the trust test</h2><p>Great answers are not enough. Explore what an AI can explain, how we validate its claims, and which measurements show whether it is dependable.</p><div class="xai-tags"><span>Understand</span><span>Verify</span><span>Measure</span><span>Monitor</span></div></section>
<section aria-labelledby="xai-introduction"><h2 id="xai-introduction">Why this matters</h2><p class="xai-lead">When an AI assistant answers a question, it may sound certain even when its answer is unsupported. Explainable AI (XAI) makes model behavior easier to examine, but understandable explanations must be paired with evidence checks, controlled evaluations, and human oversight—especially in healthcare, finance, and law.</p></section>
<figure><img class="poster" src="/parin-ai-portfolio/assets/aiml-501-xai-infographic.svg" alt="Visual overview of AI explainability, validation, metrics, evidence checks, industry approaches, and the audit loop."><figcaption class="figure-caption">Explainable AI: the trust test.</figcaption></figure>
<section aria-labelledby="xai-challenges"><div class="eyebrow">01 / Understand the limits</div><h2 id="xai-challenges">Four reasons an answer is difficult to explain</h2><div class="xai-grid4">
<div class="xai-panel challenge"><div class="xai-icon" aria-hidden="true">🧠</div><h3>Distributed complexity</h3><p>Meaning is represented across many interacting parameters, not one readable rule.</p></div>
<div class="xai-panel challenge"><div class="xai-icon" aria-hidden="true">⚠️</div><h3>Confident mistakes</h3><p>Fluent responses can include invented facts, citations, or unsupported claims.</p></div>
<div class="xai-panel challenge"><div class="xai-icon" aria-hidden="true">⚖️</div><h3>Bias and coverage</h3><p>Missing, noisy, or uneven data may lead to different error rates across groups.</p></div>
<div class="xai-panel challenge"><div class="xai-icon" aria-hidden="true">🔎</div><h3>Unfaithful accounts</h3><p>A generated step-by-step explanation may not describe the actual internal computation.</p></div>
</div></section>
<section aria-labelledby="xai-case"><div class="eyebrow">02 / Verify the evidence</div><h2 id="xai-case">A simple case: the warranty question</h2><div class="xai-contrast"><div class="quote bad"><b class="xai-bad">✕ AI ANSWER</b><p>“Yes, the warranty covers water damage.”</p></div><div class="quote good"><b class="xai-good">✓ SOURCE POLICY</b><p>“Water damage is excluded from warranty coverage.”</p></div></div><div class="xai-result">The answer is not grounded in the source. Ask for a policy citation, verify the cited section, record the error, and route uncertain cases to a person. <em>Illustrative example, not an evaluation of a real product.</em></div></section>
<section aria-labelledby="xai-metrics"><div class="eyebrow">03 / Make reliability measurable</div><h2 id="xai-metrics">Six checks for an AI evaluation scorecard</h2><p>Metrics must match the task and be interpreted with the consequences of mistakes in mind. These are measurement definitions, not performance claims for any vendor.</p><div class="xai-grid">
<div class="xai-panel xai-metric"><span class="xai-symbol">01 ↗</span><h3>Factual grounding</h3><p>What share of checked factual claims is supported by the provided evidence?</p><div class="xai-formula">Supported claims / claims reviewed</div></div>
<div class="xai-panel xai-metric"><span class="xai-symbol">02 ◉</span><h3>Task accuracy</h3><p>How often is the response correct against an independently labeled reference?</p><div class="xai-formula">Correct responses / evaluated responses</div></div>
<div class="xai-panel xai-metric"><span class="xai-symbol">03 ≋</span><h3>Robustness</h3><p>Does performance hold when wording, input quality, and context change?</p><div class="xai-formula">Test paraphrases, noise and edge cases</div></div>
<div class="xai-panel xai-metric"><span class="xai-symbol">04 ⚖</span><h3>Bias and subgroup gaps</h3><p>Are important user groups experiencing unequal false-positive or false-negative rates?</p><div class="xai-formula">Compare errors by relevant subgroups</div></div>
<div class="xai-panel xai-metric"><span class="xai-symbol">05 ⛨</span><h3>Safety failure rate</h3><p>How often does the model generate harmful or disallowed responses in relevant tests?</p><div class="xai-formula">Unsafe outputs / relevant test cases</div></div>
<div class="xai-panel xai-metric"><span class="xai-symbol">06 ↝</span><h3>Human handoff</h3><p>When evidence is missing or risk is high, does the system defer to qualified reviewers?</p><div class="xai-formula">Review and escalation coverage</div></div>
</div><p class="xai-note">Important: metrics need clear denominators, representative test sets, risk-based thresholds, and documented limitations. No single score proves a model is safe or fair.</p></section>
<section aria-labelledby="xai-validation"><div class="eyebrow">04 / Validate throughout the lifecycle</div><h2 id="xai-validation">From lab test to real-world monitoring</h2><div class="xai-steps">
<div class="xai-step"><div class="number">1</div><h3>Design tests</h3><p>Representative cases, clear labels, leakage checks</p></div>
<div class="xai-step"><div class="number">2</div><h3>Hold-out set</h3><p>Evaluate on data not used for development</p></div>
<div class="xai-step"><div class="number">3</div><h3>Stress-test</h3><p>Paraphrases, difficult inputs and bias probes</p></div>
<div class="xai-step"><div class="number">4</div><h3>Human review</h3><p>Domain experts assess consequential failures</p></div>
<div class="xai-step"><div class="number">5</div><h3>Monitor drift</h3><p>Track live errors and revalidate after changes</p></div>
</div></section>

<div class="xai-industry"><span class="xai-orgicon" aria-hidden="true">⚙️</span><strong>OpenAI</strong><span class="badge">Mechanistic research</span><p>Investigates interpretable features and sparse neural circuits; publishes system cards and evaluations.</p></div>

<div class="xai-industry"><span class="xai-orgicon" aria-hidden="true">🛡️</span><strong>Meta</strong><span class="badge">Llama documentation</span><p>Publishes Llama model cards with intended uses, safety evaluations, and documented limitations.</p></div>

<section aria-labelledby="xai-responsibility"><div class="eyebrow">06 / Responsible deployment</div><h2 id="xai-responsibility">Transparency is also about accountability</h2><p>The NIST AI Risk Management Framework connects explainability with validity, reliability, safety, and fairness. In the European Union, Article 13 of the AI Act establishes specific transparency and information requirements for <strong>high-risk AI systems</strong>. Neither framework means that every model explanation is an audit-grade proof; practitioners still need documented testing and appropriate human oversight.</p><div class="xai-takeaway">An explanation can help us ask better questions. Validation and metrics provide the evidence needed to decide whether an AI system is reliable enough for a particular use.</div></section>
<section aria-labelledby="xai-notes"><div class="eyebrow">07 / Artifact narrative</div><h2 id="xai-notes">Design and learning reflection</h2><p>Explainable AI (XAI) seeks to make AI behavior understandable and open to scrutiny, particularly when models such as GPT, Claude, Gemini, and Llama influence decisions. The infographic uses a warranty-coverage example to show why a fluent answer is not necessarily a correct one: the model claims that water damage is covered, but the source policy excludes it. The central diagram identifies four recurring obstacles: complex internal representations, convincing but unsupported responses, bias inherited from data, and explanations that may not faithfully describe a model’s computational process. These issues matter in healthcare, finance, legal services, and other settings where unverified answers can create real harm. The illustrated audit process combines evidence checks, expert review, and understandable communication rather than treating model self-explanations as proof.</p><p>The artifact presents trustworthiness as three complementary activities: explainability asks why an answer was produced; validation tests whether it works for the intended task; and performance metrics show how frequently it succeeds or fails. For language models, useful measures include the proportion of factual claims supported by evidence, task-specific accuracy, error rates across subgroups, resilience to reworded or adversarial inputs, and rates of unsafe responses or appropriate escalation. Reliable evaluation uses representative held-out cases, leakage checks, edge-case and bias tests, human assessment, and post-deployment monitoring. The industry cards distinguish research into model internals—OpenAI&#x27;s sparse features and circuits, Anthropic&#x27;s attribution graphs, and Google DeepMind&#x27;s Gemma Scope—from Meta&#x27;s practical transparency through Llama model cards and documented evaluations. I selected a visual audit loop, contrasting colors, short labels, and a familiar policy example to make these ideas accessible to nontechnical readers. The key message is that explaining an output, validating it against evidence, and measuring its reliability are all necessary; none alone guarantees trustworthy AI.</p></section>
